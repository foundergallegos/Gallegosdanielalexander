# My Journey So Far

I'm 15, and I've been building things since I was 13.

I come from Santa Ana, El Salvador, a city far removed from the major startup ecosystems. Growing up outside those environments has shaped how I've approached learning, building, and finding opportunities. I haven't had the same access to networks or resources that people in larger tech hubs might have, but that hasn't stopped me from exploring what's possible.

I'm also pursuing a technical education. I'm about to start studying Technological Infrastructure and IT Services (ITSI) at C.E. INSA, one of the technical education institutions in my city. The program covers areas such as computer systems, networking, programming logic, software development, and cybersecurity.

But my journey started somewhere completely different: video games.

## Where It All Started: Game Development

I got obsessed with creating Geometry Dash levels. At first, I received criticism, but I didn't stop.

After the game's major update, I released around 9 levels that collectively reached approximately 400K downloads and 30K likes. Some of them also made it into the game's top Featured level sections.

That experience made me realize I wanted to create my own video game.

I started building a team, bringing together people willing to collaborate without compensation. I grew the team, recruited talent, and we worked together for around two months.

During that time, I learned a lot about leadership, team coordination, and project organization, often figuring things out without relying too heavily on AI.

I realized that leadership is an incredibly valuable skill, and something I genuinely want to keep exploring.

Eventually, I found a co-founder, and now we're working on a new 3D action game, with the goal of launching a Kickstarter campaign.

One of my biggest dreams has always been to release a video game and build a company. Little by little, I'm getting closer to making both happen, even when things don't go as planned.

## Learning the Creative Side

Along the way, I also became obsessed with learning new things.

I explored narrative design and writing, developing a strong interest in storytelling and narrative structure in a relatively short amount of time.

Game design and level design also became major interests of mine. I wanted to understand not only how to build games, but also why certain experiences work and how players interact with them.

## Discovering Startups

Then I discovered the startup and business ecosystem, and I honestly think it was one of the best decisions I've made.

I dedicated a lot of time to learning about startups, business models, validation, and identifying real customer pain points.

Within just a few months, I built my first startup.

One of my biggest lessons came from a Reddit thread I published myself. I started a discussion around a problem and discovered that users were experiencing genuine frustrations with it.

Eventually, I learned that having a painful problem wasn't enough. The product also had to overcome adoption costs, especially when users already had simple workarounds.

Despite that, I managed to take the project quite far, eventually reaching an MVP that was close to production-ready.

That experience taught me a lot about validating ideas, understanding users, and recognizing when a solution isn't necessarily a viable business.

## What's Next?

Today, I'm still exploring new ideas, learning, and collaborating with other founders while working toward one of my biggest ambitions: getting into one of the world's top startup accelerators.

I'm particularly interested in AI agents, AI infrastructure, developer tools, and the broader AI ecosystem.

Coming from a city outside the major startup hubs, I know there's still a lot I need to learn and a long way to go. But I'm excited about the process.
